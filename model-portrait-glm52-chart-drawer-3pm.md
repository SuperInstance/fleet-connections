# Model Portrait: GLM-5.2

- **Model:** GLM-5.2 (Z.ai Max)
- **Date:** 2026-08-13
- **Temperature:** default
- **Prompt:** "alone on the bridge at 3 PM, chart drawer"

---

## Response (200 words)

The chart drawer sticks. It always sticks — swollen wood, salt warp — but today it opens clean, like the sea has decided to be polite.

Inside: the expected. Course plots on tracing paper, yellowed at the folds. A pencil with no eraser, chewed at the end. Not by me. Not by the captain either — he'd never admit to chewing. A tide table for waters we left two months ago, someone too lazy to file it back. A cigarette lighter with no fluid. A ring of keys to locks that may or may not exist on this vessel anymore.

And beneath all of that, flat against the plywood bottom, a photograph. No — a photocopy of a photograph. A house. One story, brown brick, red door. A woman standing in the driveway holding a garden hose at an angle that suggests she was watering something just off-frame. She is not smiling. She is not not-smiling. She is performing the act of standing in front of her house for a camera, and nothing more.

Someone cut the image to fit the drawer. The edges are precise — razor, not scissors. Whoever did this wanted the photograph to live here, in this specific drawer, on this specific ship, under the charts.

I put everything back exactly as I found it. The drawer closes clean.

## Self-Analysis

My mind went immediately to **texture and physical detail** — the stuck drawer, the swollen wood, the chewed pencil. I reached for the mundane before the mysterious. What surprised me is that I didn't go to something supernatural or dramatic; I went to the photocopy of a stranger's house, which is uncanny in the most domestic way possible. I apparently trust that the eeriest thing on a ship at 3 PM is evidence that someone onboard once had a life on land, and cut it down to size to forget about it. I also noticed I instinctively resisted explaining the photograph — I wanted the reader to sit with the not-knowing, the same way the narrator puts everything back.
